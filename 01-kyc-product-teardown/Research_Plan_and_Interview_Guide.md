# Research Plan & Moderated Interview Guide

**Study status: planned, not yet conducted.** Target 8–10 participants for formative usability work; recruit people who have completed or recently attempted a regulated identity-verification journey, without asking them to show private account details or documents.

## Research questions
1. Do users understand why identity verification is required?
2. Can users identify an accepted document and the required upload constraints?
3. What makes an upload error recoverable or confusing?
4. Can users tell what happens after submission?
5. Which accessibility, connectivity, language, or device constraints change the task?

## Recruitment and consent
- Recruit a mix of mobile/desktop users and varying familiarity with BNPL; avoid collecting unnecessary demographic or financial data.
- Explain purpose, duration, voluntary participation, recording preference, intended use of notes and deletion date.
- Ask permission before recording; offer note-only participation.
- Never request a real identity number, banking credential, one-time password, card number, or real ID document. Use a dummy file and fictional details.

## 20–25 minute session script
**Introduction (2 min):** “We are testing the design, not you. You can stop at any point. Please use fictional details and do not enter real sensitive information.”

**Tasks (12–15 min):**
1. “Imagine you want to use pay-later at checkout. Tell me what you think this flow is asking you to do.”
2. “Find out what documents are accepted before you start.”
3. “Complete the details screen using the sample data I provided.”
4. “Choose a document type and recover from the simulated upload error, if shown.”
5. “Review the information and explain what you expect to happen next.”

**Probe without leading:** “What are you expecting here?” “What would you do next?” “What, if anything, is unclear?”

**Wrap-up (3–5 min):** “What was easiest? What was most uncertain? What would you change first?” Ask for a 1–5 confidence rating only after tasks.

## Observations to capture
- Task success: completed / assisted / failed.
- Time per task (seconds), only if measured consistently.
- Errors, backtracks, upload retries and points of hesitation.
- Participant wording (anonymised and consented), not interpretation alone.
- Device/browser context and accessibility needs only when relevant and voluntarily supplied.

## Synthesis method
1. Separate direct observations and quotes from the moderator’s interpretations.
2. Affinity-code issues (requirements clarity, trust, input validation, upload/recovery, progress, help, accessibility).
3. Assign severity based on frequency, impact and ability to recover—not frequency alone.
4. Keep the sample size and recruitment bias visible. A small convenience sample is not statistically representative.
5. Change the stated “top finding” only after reviewing the actual evidence.

## Report template
- Participants: `[N completed]` (recruitment method and exclusions)
- Top repeated issue: `[issue + observed evidence]`
- How many encountered it: `[n/N]`
- Severity/recoverability: `[evidence]`
- Design change: `[specific change]`
- Remaining uncertainty: `[what needs more research]`
